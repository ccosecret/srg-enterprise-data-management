**Client:** Savanna Retail Group (SRG) | **Prepared by:** Lead Enterprise Data Management Consultant (acting red-team lead)
**Charter:** an independent, hostile reading of this portfolio's own claims. Each finding states the target (decision or assumption), the strongest attack, an honest verdict (**Survives** = attack fails on evidence; **Qualified** = claim holds only under stated conditions; **Overturned** = the portfolio must change), the counter-evidence, the mitigation now adopted, and the residual risk. No finding is dismissed for comfort.

### Deep dives on the three highest-severity findings

1. **RT-01 (notification exposure) — the portfolio's own original sin.** The red-team position: *everything else in this file is worthless if the incident remains unassessed.* The engagement's design response is that remediation is the **first** milestone (M1), not the last: encryption/MDM, SOP, legal referral, breach register, tabletop, external audit — an unbroken chain ending at the 12-month statutory horizon. Any slippage in this chain is escalated ahead of analytics slippage at every Steering Group. Residual: the original delay cannot be repaired, only disclosed and prevented.
2. **RT-02 (budget interpretation) — a solvency question, not a rounding question.** UGX 180,000,000 vs a UGX 38,350,000 contingency is the difference between a funded programme and a re-scoped one. Mitigation is procedural: **no irreversible commitment before G1** (M3), when the Board confirms payroll treatment; the deferral order (cloud → footfall pilot → third hire) is pre-agreed so a "no" becomes a smaller plan, not a crisis.
3. **RT-03 (evidence provenance) — the quiet assumption under every number.** The correct posture is not to hide the extract's origin but to *price* it: treat all current figures as pilot-grade, re-baseline from live feeds by M6, and authorise no capital decision (D-26) until independent evidence (footfall, Odoo stock, provider statements) has confirmed the same direction. If live data contradicts the extract, the roadmap's KPI targets — not the data — get rewritten.

### Think-deeper answer

**The red team attacked the strategy — who red-teams the red team, and is this critique genuine or performative?**

The honest test is whether any finding *changed* something, and three did. First, RT-01 forced sequencing: incident remediation and the notification assessment were placed ahead of every analytics milestone — had the red team not raised it, the natural momentum of a data portfolio would have produced dashboards while the original regulatory exposure sat unaddressed. Second, RT-09 forced scope: "GDPR-equivalent" was re-scoped from a claim about technical controls to an auditable programme (RoPA, breach register, lawful basis, SCCs), because default-deny RBAC protects data *in the database* but does not satisfy a European partner's evidence request. Third, RT-02 forced a procedural constraint — no irreversible commitment before gate G1 — which converts a budget ambiguity into a scheduled decision instead of a hidden assumption.

What the red team could *not* do is worth stating just as plainly: it cannot create evidence that does not exist (six months of one synthetic extract, no margin feed, no footfall panel), so several verdicts are necessarily "Qualified" rather than resolved — and a critique that always ends in "but the plan is sound" would indeed be theatre. The residual asymmetry is deliberate: the register contains more Survives/Qualified verdicts against our own claims than against the client's comfortable assumptions, because the portfolio's job is to be wrong *early and on paper* rather than late and on the balance sheet. The falsification register is the accountability mechanism: each claim has a named check and a month, which is what separates red-teaming from scepticism — anyone can doubt; the discipline is to say in advance which observation would settle the doubt.
