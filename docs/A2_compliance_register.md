### How this register is operated

- **Closure evidence:** a row closes only when the remediation artifact exists and has been demonstrated at the Council — for example CMP-05 closes on the export-approval workflow plus the RBAC test suite run, not on a statement of intent.
- **Escalation:** any P1 open beyond 30 days is reported to the Board in the quarterly report with a named owner and a recovery date.
- **Audit path (Month 12):** registration evidence (CMP-01/02), DPO designation (CMP-03), breach procedure and drill (CMP-04/09), ROPA (CMP-07), DPIA (CMP-08), POL-001..POL-003 execution records and deletion certificates constitute the PDPO audit pack.

### Think-deeper answer

*Which single policy would have prevented the Jinja incident, which is violated daily, and how is that made enforceable?*

The register answers this mechanically. **POL-001 (Access & Classification)** is the preventer: rows CMP-05 and CMP-04 both resolve to it — Restricted classification of `full_name`, `phone`, `email`, `payment_ref` and purchase history removes the "no one knew it was sensitive" defence, default-deny means no standing right to export, and the export-approval gate (more than 100 Restricted records, dual approval, encryption, 7-day expiry) intercepts the exact 9,000-record copy that left Jinja. **POL-002 (Data Quality and capture hygiene)** is the one violated daily: free-text entry, e-mailed supplier Excels, ad-hoc spreadsheets and unticketed production edits recur dozens of times a day because each is individually convenient. Its enforcement in this register is visible in the linkage: CMP-06, CMP-07 and CMP-21 are all *evidence* obligations that only survive if entry-time consent capture, ROPA fields and masking are enforced by forms, quarantine and `GRANT`s rather than by instruction — and in the closure rule that a gap is not closed until a demonstrable control exists.
