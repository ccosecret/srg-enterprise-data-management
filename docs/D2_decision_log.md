### Think-deeper answer

**Which decision in this log is most likely to be wrong, and why does it remain the right decision anyway?**

**D-17 — deferring all machine learning while the firm's churn proxy sits at 69.4%** is the most vulnerable call: a challenger could argue that with 180,000 loyalty members and a visibly bleeding customer base, a propensity model is precisely what a competitor would deploy first, and that "descriptive first" is consultant caution dressed as method. The counter-argument is not that ML would fail technically — it is that *SRG cannot currently verify its own inputs*: 8.7% of revenue has no customer attached, 11.5% of mobile-money lines have no payment reference, and 8.4% of stock variance is unexplained. A churn model trained through those holes would produce confident scores attached to customers who may not exist (23% were duplicates until M2) and would be un-auditable to a Board that cannot yet answer "how do you know?" — the very question this engagement was commissioned to fix.

The decision is also structured to be *reversible at known cost*: D-25 makes the pilots a gated option (cost of waiting ≈ zero; cost of being wrong ≈ one quarter of analyst time plus Board credibility). Contrast it with **D-01 (incident response)**, which is irreversible in the other direction — silence already happened and cannot be un-happened — and with **D-07 (no name-only merges)**, where a wrong auto-merge cannot be cleanly undone because overwritten identity history destroys a person's record. The log's ordering principle is: *whichever way new evidence falls, the cost of reversing this decision stays smaller than the cost of having been wrong*. That is the test every entry above was kept against — and it is why D-17 survives challenge while remaining the first item to re-examine at gate G3, once a full year of cleansed history exists.
