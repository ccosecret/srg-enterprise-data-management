# Part D — Synthesis, Defense & Reflection

## Task D1 — Integrated 18-Month EDM Strategy Roadmap

**Mandate.** The roadmap integrates the diagnostic baseline, the six workstreams and the governance model into a single 18-month sequence that closes every defect the assessment found: an unremediated breach, 23% duplicate customers, five product identifiers, an 11-day reporting lag and an 8.4% stock variance. It binds milestones, dependencies and gates to a non-negotiable envelope of UGX 480,000,000 — all-in for licences, cloud and hires — an IT team of four plus three planned hires, and a regulatory horizon in which the PDPO audit must fall inside 12 months alongside a European distributor partnership requiring GDPR-equivalent practice. Nothing is scheduled for its own sake: each phase ends in a binary gate, so evidence — not elapsed time — releases the next tranche of work, spend and headcount, and the M18 target state is one conformed warehouse on a ≤3-day reporting lag, quality prevented at entry, and an operating model of four staff, three hires and five business stewards running analytics without external help.

Read strictly, UGX 480,000,000 is the **18-month programme total**: the envelope is stated annually in the scenario, but the roadmap plans to the stricter reading (480,000,000 ÷ 18 = UGX 26,666,667 per month) and no commitment assumes the upside of the annual reading. At 1 USD ≈ UGX 3,700 the envelope is roughly USD 129,730 for the whole programme of hires, licences, cloud, integration, security and compliance — and Year 1 (Phase 1 plus Phase 2) draws 333,375,000, cloud sits at 60,000,000 under its 90,000,000 ceiling, and licences total 15,000,000, so the plan also survives the looser annual reading without modification. Contingency of 38,350,000 (8.0%) is the residual after 441,650,000 of itemised commitment and is the only slack in the plan. Gate G1 (M3) must confirm payroll treatment of the existing 4 IT staff — carried by this budget they would cost 4 × 2,500,000 × 18 = 180,000,000, exceeding contingency by 141,650,000 and forcing a re-scope; the standing assumptions behind that figure sit in Appendix A.

**Risk mitigation and success metrics.** Seven risks carry named owners and pre-agreed responses: a market undershoot on the 3,500,000 data-engineer salary is met by one senior hire plus two upskilled internal staff, with contract support funded from contingency; a cloud overrun is contained by the 90,000,000 cap and a 5,000,000 monthly spend alarm; scope creep into Kenya or Rwanda before G4 is blocked to data-model readiness only; key-person dependence is diluted by documentation-as-code, pair review and stewardship distributed to business roles; a PDPO finding on the original theft is met by the M1 legal assessment and a breach register built before the audit; quality regression at entry fires a P1 incident when DQC-01 exceeds 1.0% for three consecutive days; and off-sample Board decisions are prevented by a provenance footnote on every artefact plus a re-baseline from live feeds by M6. Pipeline integrity is binary — the dashboard build fails if the warehouse control total ≠ UGX 252,382,814.16 — and the KPI trajectory below is the scorecard the gates are read against.

**Dependencies and governance checkpoints.** Four dependencies gate the plan — recruitment at an 8–10 week lead time with offers M3–M5, Odoo stock-feed access by M8, MTN MoMo and Airtel Money statement exports from M5, and an auditor booked by M6 for the M12 slot — while power and connectivity are managed continuously through UPS windows, idempotent replay and advisory-lock scheduling. The gates convert those dependencies into binary decisions: **G1 (M3)** adopts the platform verdict, publishes triggers T1–T6, confirms the RBAC and masking test suite green, and settles payroll treatment of the existing four IT staff; **G2 (M6)** accepts or rejects the eight quick wins as Board evidence; **G3 (M12)** releases Phase 3 only if the PDPO audit has passed, reporting sits at ≤3 days, stock variance at ≤2.0% and pending mobile-money below 0.5%; **G4 (M18)** re-scores maturity at L3, takes the expansion decision on footfall-backed evidence and commissions roadmap v2.

**Coherence.** The sequence is one causal chain, not six parallel projects. Governance feeds quality: the Council sets metric definitions and rule changes, stewards clear the queue against a 30-day SLA, and DQC-01 to DQC-10 are the only mechanism keeping entry-point discipline from decaying. Quality enables master data: with duplicates below 1.0% and one phone format, survivorship can produce a golden customer key and a golden product key — what turns 5,000 records into 3,858 and five identifiers into one. Master data enables integration: the Odoo feed, the mobile-money reconciliation and the e-commerce node can only land against keys that mean the same thing in every system, so the star schema loads in days rather than weeks. Metadata sustains the result — lineage, catalog and `etl_run_log` make ownership and freshness visible once external support ends. Security wraps the same records the model depends on, and analytics monetises them: a ≤3-day lag plus the five Board questions turn controlled data into double-down lists, margin decisions and a footfall-backed expansion call. Each gate then restates the chain for the Board in the monthly brief, so a slip anywhere surfaces as a gate failure rather than a surprise at M18.

**Think deeper.** Halve the envelope to UGX 240,000,000 and the plan changes shape rather than shrinking evenly: People (150,650,000) and Security (72,000,000) alone consume 222,650,000, leaving 17,350,000 for cloud, foundation, licences, governance and contingency combined. The sacrifice ladder then applies in order — cloud stays on-premises, the footfall pilot slides and the expansion decision falls back on district and loyalty indices, the third hire is deferred, and Foundation collapses to the interim POS stock-ledger stop-gap. What a halved budget cannot change is the obligation set: incident remediation, the daily DQC checks and the PDPO audit date survive intact, because scope is negotiable and statutory duties are not — the envelope is contestable, the licence to operate is not.

**Red-team critique.** Full red-team dossier (15 findings): Appendix AA.

The target is SRG's own strategy and the evidence supporting it. Verdicts follow the dossier's convention: **Survives** = the attack fails on the evidence; **Qualified** = the claim holds only under stated conditions; **Overturned** = the claim must change.

**1. Budget — Qualified (RT-02, RT-12).** The UGX 480,000,000 envelope balances only because it silently assumes the existing four IT staff stay on the corporate payroll. Move them inside the "all-in" line — 4 × 2,500,000 × 18 = 180,000,000 — and the programme is short by 141,650,000 against a contingency of 38,350,000: insolvent as written. Optimism bias compounds it: six workstreams, three hires, an audit, an Odoo feed and a footfall pilot in 18 months, run by the team that let an ETL fail for four days unwatched. The rebuttal is procedural: the assumption is disclosed, nothing irreversible is committed before G1, and the deferral order — cloud, then footfall pilot, then third hire — is pre-agreed. The verdict stays Qualified: two readings of the brief coexist until M3.

**2. Change resistance — Survives, durability unproven (RT-11, RT-15).** The hostile reading: quality was fixed by a batch cleanse, not by a process. The M7 till phone-capture change re-opens the entry point that created 23% duplicates, free-text habits (six phone formats, 127 district labels) return the moment dropdowns annoy staff, and 110 queued records can rot past their 30-day SLA. Survives, because the control detects exactly that regression — DQC-01 above 1.0% for three days is a P1 incident, VR-001 and VR-007 are enforced at capture, and "0.00%" is reported as state on golden records with capability measured separately. Concession: the first live intake month will likely breach 1.0% briefly, and the brief's targets depend on Marketing executing from M7 — tracked as a G2 exit dependency.

**3. Connectivity — Survives, already priced (RT-08, RT-14).** No cloud region exists in Uganda: latency runs to South Africa or Europe, egress invoices cannot be forecast by a four-person team, and cloud does nothing for stores that drop offline for hours daily. On the weighted matrix connectivity scored 3 for every vendor, which makes the Azure verdict look like preference dressed as analysis; reporting-lag promises also collide with the nightly offline replay window. Both survive because Phase 1 never left the premises — cloud capped at 90,000,000 with a 5,000,000 monthly spend alarm, trigger T5 comparing real quotes against the run-rate, advisory lock and schedule window proven, lag measured from `etl_run_log`. Concession: an egress surprise at migration and occasional >3-day months remain possible.

**4. Staff skills — Qualified (RT-12).** The attack is blunt: 3,500,000 per month will not buy a senior data engineer, the firm starts with zero data-engineering capability, every BI artefact has a single owner, and a Gantt chart cannot show that nobody here has done this before. Qualified, because the gap is priced rather than wished away: 150,650,000 (31.4%) buys three hires, 18,000,000 buys training, and the mitigation is one senior hire plus two upskilled internal staff, staggered offers from M1 and stewardship distributed to business roles. Residual: if recruitment fails outright, the post goes to an internal SQL-capable promotion funded inside contingency, and the steward hire waits.

**5. Political capture of governance — Overturned in part, otherwise Qualified (RT-10, RT-13, RT-03, RT-09, RT-01).** Gates G2 to G4 are approved by the Board that wants a Kenya announcement, so who governs the governors? City indices of 0.94–1.04 have two readings — a balanced estate or an absence of measurement — yet the flat reading is used against Kampala densification; category rankings by revenue may crown the least profitable category while no margin feed exists until M14, and boards rarely un-hear a polished ranking; every figure rests on one six-month extract carrying seasonality artefacts such as the 69.4% churn proxy. Two findings are **Overturned**: "GDPR-equivalent" as a claim about RBAC and masking — RoPA, breach register, lawful basis and SCCs were forced into Phase 2 — and the original notification exposure, outranked by analytics until red-teaming forced it to M1. **Qualified** on expansion and evidence: no capex before G4, both scorecard readings shown, provenance footnoted, margin caveat printed.

#### The reflective essay

**The assumption that reframed the engagement**

I arrived believing SRG's problem was technical: 23% duplicates, five identifiers per product, an 11-day reporting lag, a failing nightly job. The Jinja laptop corrected me — an unencrypted export of 9,000 customer records stolen, reported internally 11 days late, never notified. The problem was never that the data was dirty; it was that nobody owned it. Every artefact I built afterwards is the *evidence* of that ownership, not merely a fix.

**Uganda's digital transformation, and what it demands of design**

SRG trades in a Uganda where mobile money leapfrogged card rails: 73.2% of revenue now flows through MTN MoMo and Airtel Money — financial inclusion infrastructure built by telecoms, while business intelligence infrastructure barely exists. That asymmetry shaped my choices. I automated reconciliation against provider statements before building churn models, because payments were where value leaked (849 pending references, ≈UGX 21.2M). The Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 is young, the PDPO's enforcement capacity is still forming, and no mature precedent tells a Ugandan retailer what "adequate" looks like — so I designed to the statute's explicit asks (register, notify *immediately*, document) rather than to a comfortable norm. Infrastructure shaped design too: hours-daily connectivity loss and unreliable power are not edge cases here, so the pipeline treats offline capture as a *designed state* — quarantine rather than abort, idempotent replay, `customer_sk = 0` for walk-ins instead of fabricated identities. Offline-first is not a compromise in Kampala; it is fit for context. The Kenya and Rwanda expansion taught the corollary: data models travel, legal regimes do not — portability of schema, not of compliance.

**Data ethics, by the four choices they forced**

**Consent in the loyalty programme.** With 180,000 members, the tempting design bundles points with marketing consent. I refused: consent must be recorded as a first-class, revocable attribute (DPPA s.7, GDPR Art.7), separable from contract performance for points (Art.6(1)(b)), with withdrawal honoured downstream in CRM, not just in a preferences table nobody reads. The credibility test is whether a member's "no" actually stops a campaign.

**Footfall sensor surveillance.** The pilot design counts bodies, not identities: aggregate entrance/zone counts, dwell-time distributions, no facial recognition, no individual tracking, signage at store entrances, DPIA signed before hardware is installed. The ethics line is that the *store* is measured, not the *shopper or the stock clerk*. A cheap camera upgrade to individual recognition will be available and will look technically interesting; the governance charter is what will keep requiring a fresh decision before it.

**Algorithmic bias in matching rules.** Automated merge rules are where ethics quietly becomes arithmetic. Name-similarity ≥90 looks neutral until you note that mononyms and common surnames inflate similarity, and shared family phones mean rural and lower-income households are structurally over-merged — two people collapsed into one, with loyalty value and purchase history reassigned. That is why name similarity alone never auto-merges (identifier match required), why 365 homonym pairs were *correctly rejected*, and why 110 pairs sit in a stewardship queue: a false merge destroys one person's record irreversibly, so the asymmetric cost justifies the human gate.

**AI fairness.** Deferring machine learning (D-25) was partly an ethics decision, not only a competence one. A churn-propensity model trained on data where 8.7% of revenue is unattributed and 11.5% of payments unreconciled would learn *measurement gaps as behaviour* — and those gaps are not random: walk-in, cash-preferring, outage-affected customers are systematically the least measured, so any future scoring would encode their poverty as disengagement. Before any pilot ships, I committed to parity checks across districts and gender, human review before account-affecting decisions (GDPR Art.22), and published model card with known blind spots.

**Career path, mapped to CDMP**

My intended path is consultancy → **CDMP Associate within 12 months, then Professional**, with a longer aim of contributing to Uganda's PDPO-era capacity — the country needs practitioners who can explain Art.33 timelines to a store manager, not only lawyers who cite them. The mapping is direct: **Data Governance** (charter, RACI, POL-001/002/003), **Data Quality** (VR-001…012, DQC-01…10, before/after evidence), **Data Modeling & Architecture** (star schema, ER model, 1NF→3NF), **Data Integration** (repaired ETL, quarantine, idempotency), **Security & Privacy** (RLS RBAC, masking, DPIA, breach plan), **Master Data** (golden record, survivorship), **BI & Analytics** (five questions, dashboard, insight brief), and **Metadata** (lineage, catalog decision) — which is also where my self-identified gap sits, and the reason the catalog pilot is on my own development plan, not only SRG's.

**What I would carry forward**

Two habits. First, honesty as a scorecard: 2.85% open quality flags reported instead of a fabricated 0%, "Stable (dedupe denominator shift)" instead of dressed-up improvement — credibility lives in the caveats. Second, sequencing: compliance milestones before analytics milestones, every time. Behind every unattributed line and pending payment reference is a customer who trusted the firm with a phone number — the same trust 9,000 of them gave, and lost, when a laptop left the Jinja store unencrypted. That is the sentence I will keep testing against every plan I write.
